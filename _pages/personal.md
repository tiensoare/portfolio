Outside of research, I am a pencil artist, a proud former cat parent, and a firm believer that there is no single "right" path into computing. Here's a little more about the journey that brought me here and the things that keep me inspired along the way.

### My Journey :seedling:

I was born and raised in **Vietnam**, a beautiful S-shaped country in Southeast Asia. Growing up in a culture where academic performance is every student's top priority, coming to the U.S. for college was a complete culture shock. The two education systems felt worlds apart: Vietnam emphasized exams and memorization, while U.S. classrooms encouraged me to ask questions, debate, and explore on my own. Learning to thrive in both taught me to value discipline and curiosity in equal measure.

My path to a Ph.D. began at a community college, **Century College**. Community colleges are often overlooked, but I believe they are a wonderful opportunity for students who are still discovering their paths, who need practical degrees for their careers, who want to save money, or who thrive in a small, close-knit environment. That was where my confidence took root.

I then transferred to the **University of Saint Thomas** on a generous scholarship, where I continued to grow and broaden my horizons in a bigger, more diverse world. Around that time, **my mom** :heart: encouraged me to consider academia. She had always seen a teacher in me, long before I saw it in myself. With her belief and the guidance of a professor who generously helped me through the application process, I took the leap toward a Ph.D.

Today, I am pursuing my Ph.D. at **Virginia Tech**, where I study how large language models reason about software and numbers. Looking back, I am still amazed that the student who arrived unsure of the system now helps others navigate it. As a teaching assistant for introductory Python and Java courses, I often see my younger self in students who are new to programming, new to the country, or simply unsure whether they belong in computing.

### Giving Back :handshake:

My own path would not have been possible without mentors who took the time to guide me, and I want to do the same for others. I am involved in diversity-in-computing communities and was honored to receive a travel scholarship to the Grace Hopper Celebration 2026. I am especially passionate about mentoring underrepresented women in tech and students from non-traditional paths, like community college transfers and international students. If that describes you, feel free to reach out. I would love to chat!

### Fun Facts :sparkles:

- **I almost gave up on coding before I started.** My first encounter with programming was a high school class in `Pascal`, and I was completely lost. It felt like an alien language :grin:. Ironically, I still entered college as a computer science major. Then I took an introductory `Python` course and fell in love. There was something magical about writing a few lines of code, hitting run, and watching my ideas come to life on the screen.

- **I once had a 21-pound cat.** His name was `Neko`, which means "cat" in Japanese, and I was always proud to tell people about my giant fur baby :cat:. He has since crossed the rainbow bridge, but he will always have a place in my heart :rainbow:.

### My Art World :art:

My favorite subjects are portraits of women in traditional and national costumes from around the world. I love the challenge of capturing intricate embroidery, layered fabrics, and ornate headdresses in nothing but graphite, and each piece sends me down a rabbit hole learning about the history behind the clothing.
